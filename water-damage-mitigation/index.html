The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. Repair is a different job. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. In Midwest City, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. In fact there are two separate stages. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Midwest City Restoration:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Signs You Need Water Damage Mitigation in Midwest City

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trade rubber washer hoses for braided steel, and renew them roughly every five years.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
