Filing an insurance claim for water damage can be overwhelming, especially when you are already dealing with a flooded or damaged property. Knowing how the claims process works, what is typically covered, and how to protect your claim can make a real difference in your financial recovery. What's Typically Covered

Most standard homeowner's insurance policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental. That category takes in failed appliances like water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines, along with sudden HVAC failures, accidental tub or sink overflows, ice dams, and burst pipes. In those cases a policy usually pays for extraction, drying the structure, replacing damaged materials, and other restoration work, plus temporary living costs if the home is unlivable.

What's Typically NOT Covered

The usual exclusions are sewage backup unless you carry a specific endorsement, outside flooding unless you hold separate flood insurance, groundwater seeping through the foundation, slow leaks and other gradual damage, and anything traced to neglected or deferred maintenance. If you learn about the gaps ahead of time, you can close them with an endorsement or a separate policy.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Report the loss to your insurer as soon as you can. Do what you reasonably can to keep the damage from getting worse (mitigation). Keep damaged items until the adjuster has inspected them. Hire a restoration company that holds IICRC certification. Living expenses, temporary repairs, and any other cost connected to the loss should each have a saved receipt. Be present during the adjuster's inspection and point out all damage.
