Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. For flood emergencies anywhere in Midwest City, Midwest City Restoration is available 24/7 to extract the water, disinfect affected areas and bring the property back safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Then the drying begins: high-velocity air movers and commercial dehumidifiers of the LGR (low-grain refrigerant) type go in to pull moisture out of the building's structural parts. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. From start to finish we document each step for your insurance claim, with the moisture maps, equipment logs, photos and scope-of-work reports adjusters ask for.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Flooring that squelches when you walk on it and walls damp to the touch
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer

Maintenance Tips for Flood Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear leaves and debris from storm drains and gutters, which lets water drain the way it should
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Elevate appliances and electrical systems in flood-prone parts of your property

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.
