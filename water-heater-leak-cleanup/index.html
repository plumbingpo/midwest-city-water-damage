Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Midwest City

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Midwest City, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Our crew extracts the water standing in the closet, the garage or on the floor, plus the overflow pan and the cavity above the ceiling on attic jobs, so that materials around it quit absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.
